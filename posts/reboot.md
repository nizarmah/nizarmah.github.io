I learned to build stuff.
On the way, I lost the ability to share it.
I'm trying to get it back.

I started out as an impostor.

As a kid, I joined the MCPE forum.
I built mods out of others' snippets.
I shared them on [YouTube](https://youtu.be/LmG0S3KPc2k).
In about a year, I had [2.9K subs and 713K views](https://imgur.com/a/7hBR3Kg).

We all shared a single laptop at home.
So I made a JS IDE for my Nexus 7.
It hit [20K downloads](https://imgur.com/a/jside-statistics-aNQexcv).

I still couldn't code.
I avoided Rhino, a JS Engine, because it was complex.
I built the IDE on a WebView and its console.

Enough copy-paste, and it started making sense.
I had momentum and was having fun with projects.

I started writing about how I built things.
I wrote about a real-time face recognition app.
It was read by [a few](https://imgur.com/a/NHE6imp) and ended up in a university course.

I never asked whether any of it was interesting.
I made things and showed people, without overthinking.

When I got better, the trick stopped working.
The more I knew, the harder I judged what I made.

Things I built went nowhere.
Open source and SaaS projects never took off.
Even if they did, I couldn't charge for it in Lebanon.

I felt like I had no way to win.
I felt like I was screaming into the void.
I felt like I was burning out.

So I went scorched earth.
I deleted all my social profiles.

Eventually, I found a good job at Sticker Mule.
I spent years unlearning how I work and communicate.

I tried to come back.
I just never held my breath long enough.

Now, I have a new list of things to write about.
And I finally have the patience to see this through.
